Research at LSU

  • A Variationist Study of Spanish Intensifiers
    Mentor: Dr. Rafael Orozco · Fall 2023 - present

    A multivariate study of 7,835 intensifier tokens across Medellín, Tunja (Colombia), and Louisiana Puerto Rican Spanish, examining how gender, age, education, and adjective type condition intensifier choice. I developed a custom Python corpus pipeline to extract and classify tokens from sociolinguistic interview transcripts. Findings show periphrastic intensification dominates, and súper is an ongoing innovation led by young women of higher socioeconomic status. Published in the Proceedings of the Linguistic Society of America 11(1). doi:10.3765/plsa.v11i1.6091

  • The Effect of Sociophonetic Variation on Listeners' Perception of English Interdental Fricatives
    Mentors: Dr. Hyunju Chung & Dr. Irina Shport · Spring 2024 - present

    Investigated whether stereotypical associations between interdental fricative variation (stopping and labialization) and talker race influence listener perception of ethnolect (AAE vs. Southern White English) across 108 listeners. I categorized stimuli, built the Qualtrics survey, and contributed to Praat acoustic analysis. Results show listeners use socio-ethnic stereotypes in speech perception, with word-final labialization serving as a stronger marker of sounding Black than word-initial stopping. Manuscript submitted to the Journal of Speech, Language, and Hearing Research; currently under review.
